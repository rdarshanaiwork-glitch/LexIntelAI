# Precedent: Lawson v. FMR LLC
COURT: Supreme Court of the United States
CITATION: 571 U.S. 429 (2014)
DATE: 2014-03-04
JURISDICTION: US Federal / Whistleblower Protection

## Facts
Plaintiffs Jackie Hosang Lawson and Jonathan M. Zang were former employees of FMR LLC (Fidelity Investments), private companies that contract with the Fidelity family of mutual funds to provide investment management services. Mutual funds themselves are public companies with no employees of their own; they contract out all operations. Plaintiffs alleged that after raising concerns about cost-accounting methodologies and mutual fund registration statements, they suffered unlawful retaliation resulting in constructive discharge.

## Legal Issue
Whether Section 806 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (18 U.S.C. § 1514A), which prohibits retaliation against an "employee" who reports corporate fraud, shelters employees of private contractors and subcontractors that advise or serve public mutual funds.

## Holding and Rule of Law
Justice Ginsburg delivered the opinion of the Court (6-3):
1. Whistleblower protection under 18 U.S.C. § 1514A extends to employees of contractors and subcontractors of public companies, including employees of private investment advisers who manage public mutual funds.
2. The ordinary meaning of "an employee" who may not be discriminated against by "any contractor, subcontractor, or agent" includes the contractor's own employees.
3. Congress enacted SOX in direct response to the Enron collapse, where outside contractors (auditors, law firms, accounting partners) were deeply implicated in concealing corporate malfeasance. Protecting contractor employees is essential to preventing retaliatory silencing of insiders who uncover shareholder fraud.
