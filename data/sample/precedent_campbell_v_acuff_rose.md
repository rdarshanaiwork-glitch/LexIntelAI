# Precedent: Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.
COURT: Supreme Court of the United States
CITATION: 510 U.S. 569 (1994)
DATE: 1994-03-07
JURISDICTION: US Federal / Copyright

## Facts
2 Live Crew created a parody rap song based on Roy Orbison and William Dees's copyrighted rock ballad "Oh, Pretty Woman". Acuff-Rose Music sued 2 Live Crew for copyright infringement after refusing to grant a license.

## Legal Issue
Whether 2 Live Crew's commercial parody constitutes fair use under Section 107 of the Copyright Act of 1976.

## Holding and Judicial Reasoning
Justice Souter delivered the opinion of the unanimous Court:
1. Parody, like other comment or criticism, may claim fair use under Section 107.
2. The enquiry under § 107(1) focuses on whether the new work merely supersedes the objects of the original creation, or instead adds something new, with a further purpose or different character; in other words, whether and to what extent the new work is "transformative".
3. The threshold question when fair use is invoked in a parody is whether a parodic character may reasonably be perceived.
4. Commercial purpose is only one element to be weighed in a fair use inquiry and does not create an irrebuttable presumption against fair use.
