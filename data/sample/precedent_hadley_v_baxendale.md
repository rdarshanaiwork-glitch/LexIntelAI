# Precedent: Hadley v Baxendale
COURT: Court of Exchequer (England & Wales)
CITATION: (1854) 9 Exch 341, 156 Eng. Rep. 145
DATE: 1854-02-23
JURISDICTION: Common Law / Commercial Contracts

## Facts
Plaintiffs operated a flour mill in Gloucester. The crankshaft of the steam engine broke, causing the mill to stop. Plaintiffs engaged Defendant Baxendale, a common carrier, to transport the broken shaft to engineers in Greenwich to serve as a pattern for a new shaft. Defendant's servant promised delivery within two days. Due to Defendant's neglect, delivery was delayed for seven days, during which the mill remained idle and lost substantial profits.

## Legal Issue
Whether a common carrier can be held liable for consequential lost business profits resulting from delivery delays when the carrier was not expressly informed that the entire operation of the mill was halted awaiting the replacement.

## Holding and Rule of Law
Where two parties have made a contract which one of them has broken, the damages which the other party ought to receive in respect of such breach of contract should be such as may fairly and reasonably be considered either arising naturally, i.e., according to the usual course of things, from such breach of contract itself, or such as may reasonably be supposed to have been in the contemplation of both parties, at the time they made the contract, as the probable result of the breach of it.

Special or consequential damages are recoverable only if the special circumstances were communicated to and known by both parties at the formation of the agreement.
