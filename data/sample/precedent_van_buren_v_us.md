# Precedent: Van Buren v. United States
COURT: Supreme Court of the United States
CITATION: 593 U.S. 374 (2021)
DATE: 2021-06-03
JURISDICTION: US Federal / Computer Fraud & Cyber Law

## Facts
Nathan Van Buren, a former police sergeant in Georgia, accepted an illicit cash payment to search a state license plate database (the Criminal Justice Information Services database) for an individual in exchange for personal financial gain. Van Buren had valid credentials and legitimate administrative access to the database as part of his police duties, but accessed the record for an unauthorized personal/improper purpose in violation of police department policy.

## Legal Issue
Whether a person "exceeds authorized access" under Section 1030(a)(2) of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act by accessing computer information with valid credentials but for an improper or unauthorized purpose.

## Holding and Rule of Law
Justice Barrett delivered the opinion of the Court (6-3):
1. An individual "exceeds authorized access" under 18 U.S.C. § 1030(e)(6) when he accesses a computer with authorization and then obtains information located in particular areas of the computer (such as files, folders, or databases) that are off-limits to him.
2. The CFAA does NOT cover an individual who has lawful administrative credentials to access information stored on a computer, even if that individual accesses that information for an improper purpose or in violation of corporate employment policies.
3. The statute establishes a "gates-up / gates-down" technological inquiry: either the technological system authorizes the user to enter that directory, or it does not. Purpose-based contractual violations (e.g. Terms of Service, employee handbooks) do not constitute unauthorized access under federal cyber law.
